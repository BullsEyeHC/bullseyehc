The hiring challenge

Security talent is not one-size-fits-all.

A product security engineer, a cloud security architect and a detection engineer may all have “cybersecurity” on their resume. Their day-to-day work, technical depth and impact can be completely different.

Effective recruiting starts by clarifying your environment, threat exposure, engineering workflows and where a hire must take ownership. The right person might secure code early in development, strengthen cloud architecture, investigate incidents, improve identity controls or build the security function itself.

A specialized recruiting discipline

Operational Technology (OT) Security Recruiting

Industrial cybersecurity is not conventional enterprise IT security. The work touches physical processes, safety, operational uptime and equipment that cannot always be patched or taken offline.

BullsEye! has experience recruiting in OT security. We understand why an OT security hire needs more than a general cybersecurity background.

Relevant searches may span industrial control systems (ICS), SCADA environments, industrial networks, plant and facility security, OT asset visibility, network segmentation, vulnerability management and OT incident detection and response.

We distinguish candidates who understand production operations, industrial protocols and safety and availability constraints from those whose security experience is limited to traditional IT environments.

Examples of roles: OT Security Engineer, ICS/SCADA Security Specialist, Industrial Cybersecurity Architect, OT Network Security Engineer and OT Security Leader.

Precision in the search brief

Start with the risk. Define the responsibility.

What needs protecting?

Customer data, software products, cloud infrastructure, enterprise systems and connected environments all call for different security expertise.

Build, defend or govern?

Hands-on engineering, operational defense, security architecture and risk oversight should not be combined into one unrealistic job description.

How does security work with engineering?

We consider the candidate's ability to collaborate with developers, platform teams and leadership while making sound tradeoffs.

What evidence shows capability?

Beyond credentials and tool names, look at systems owned, incidents handled, technical decisions, improvements delivered and scope of responsibility.
